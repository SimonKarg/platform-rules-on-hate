How platforms write their rules on hate

Have you ever looked at the Community Guidelines of a social media platform and wondered what they are about?

Almost all social platforms lay out so called Community Guidelines that describe the overall vision the platform has about itself and the type of content that they wish to be present. These pages communicate the kinds of content platforms do not want to see, and the rules they have in place to remove such content. They define what counts hate speech, who is especially protected, and what is allowed to be said in response.

Community rules are long, and frequently re-written, often without any announcement. Most social media users have never read the rules, and many don't even know they exist. This project looks at the Community Guidelines of nine social media platforms, and how they have changed over time (from 2022 to today).

The three pages look at the same rules from three angles:

  1. how the rules define hate, whom they protect, and how this has changed over time
  2. how the rules talk about counterspeech
  3. how changing platform rules affect what is allowed and what is not
This site builds on the amazing data source collected by the Open Terms Archive, which has archived every version of many (more than analysed here) platform guidelines, terms of service, etc. for an extended period of time.

The data

Every archived version of each platform's Community Guidelines since 2022, from Open Terms Archive's Platform Governance Archive, filled in with Wayback Machine captures where it has gaps.

What it shows

The written rules, and how they changed. Not how the rules are enforced: what moderators and automated systems actually remove is a different question.

The live judge

The arena's "Write your own" box sends your text to a small Azure Function, which asks Zentropi's CoPE model to apply the rules you picked. Nothing is stored.
